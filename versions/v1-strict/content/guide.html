Why do mosquitoes bite in the first place?

Not all mosquito species target humans, and among the ones that do, only the females bite. A female mosquito needs the protein and iron found in blood to develop her eggs. The hunt begins when she tracks your exhaled carbon dioxide (CO2), body heat, and unique skin scent. Once she lands, she searches for exposed skin and uses specialized mouthparts to pierce it. As she feeds, she injects saliva containing anticoagulants and mild anesthetics so you don’t feel the initial bite.

Later, your immune system reacts to the proteins in that saliva, triggering the inflammation, redness, and itch you know all too well.

One bite can carry more than an itch

When a mosquito bites, her sharp needle-like mouthpart (called a proboscis) goes straight into your skin. Germs and tiny parasites can live inside this tube and slip right into your blood before you even notice. You may already know about West Nile Virus or Malaria, but mosquitoes also carry lesser-known threats like Dengue, Chikungunya, and Eastern Equine Encephalitis. These illnesses can cause high fevers, painful joint aches, and even dangerous brain swelling. Just one bite can put a healthy person in the hospital, but you can block these pests before they reach you. Essential protection includes bed nets for night, stroller netting for baby strollers, strong insect repellents, outdoor bug zappers, smoke coils, and clothing sprays containing Permethrin, which kills mosquitoes on contact when they land.

Three layers, because one layer fails alone

  • Layer 1: Block

    Nets and screens keep mosquitoes away from skin. You are here.

  • Layer 2: Reduce

    Repellents, traps, and standing-water control cut how many are around.

  • Layer 3: Relieve

    Creams and balms calm the bites that still get through.

A net can’t follow you outdoors, and repellent wears off overnight. Each layer covers the other’s gap.
